My research provides computational support for ergonomic risk management across two phases of manual work: predicting working postures before a task or workstation exists, and assessing postural and biomechanical exposure once it does.

Research

Two complementary tools are developed and evaluated: a hybrid two-phase posture prediction model for the design phase, and an online video-based ergonomic assessment system for the evaluation phase. The markerless measurement technology on which the system depends is validated first, the system is developed on that basis, and the system’s outputs are then evaluated against established reference methods and with expert end users.

As a research assistant, I am the lead developer of a markerless system for human movement analysis from ordinary single-camera video, requiring no markers, force plates or laboratory set-up, and supporting gait analysis, manual-handling risk assessment with lumbar loading estimation, and preliminary screening in rehabilitation assessment.

This work builds on my master’s research at Beijing Sport University in China on the effects of the bending stiffness of racing running shoes on the running economy and lower-limb biomechanical characteristics of runners.

Digital human modelling · Doctoral research

A hybrid two-phase framework for posture prediction of manual material handling tasks

This study developed a hybrid two-phase modelling approach combining sagittal-plane whole-body optimisation with analytical 3D upper-limb reconstruction. Phase I employed a 5-degree-of-freedom (DOF) constrained nonlinear optimisation that minimised the trunk moment, subject to task and biomechanical constraints. Phase II analytically reconstructed 4-DOF upper-limb posture through inverse kinematics with empirically derived humeral rotation constraints. Posture predictions were compared with marker-based motion capture in twelve participants performing vertical lifting, and shoulder-height and above-shoulder drilling. Joint-angle RMSE ranged from 4.0° to 37.5°, with a total computation time of approximately 85 ms per posture. The modular architecture and sub-100 ms computation time support integration into digital human modelling workflows and early-stage ergonomic design.

Markerless motion capture · Doctoral research

Concurrent validity of markerless motion capture for occupational lifting: Kinematics and ergonomic risk assessment

This study compared a multi-view system (OpenCap) and a monocular system (MediaPipe) against a marker-based reference system (Vicon) for lifting kinematics. Twenty healthy participants performed three two-handed lifting tasks of increasing complexity under controlled laboratory conditions. Errors increased by 15–30% from symmetrical to asymmetric multi-planar tasks. Both systems were reliable for symmetrical lifting, including high REBA agreement, but validity declined for tasks involving trunk rotation and walking.

Video-based ergonomic assessment · Doctoral research

From clipboard to camera: an online video-based ergonomic assessment system for New Zealand workplaces

An online video-based ergonomic assessment system that analyses workplace tasks from a single standard video using markerless pose estimation, removing the need for markers, wearable sensors, force plates or a laboratory. Four modules run from the same video: RULA and REBA postural scoring; NIOSH Recommended Weight Limit and Lifting Index; static joint moments and L5/S1 spinal compression; and dynamic biomechanics across full movement cycles. REBA risk categories agreed with the reference in 84% of 132 trials, the NIOSH Lifting Index showed a mean absolute error of 0.13, and trunk moment and L5/S1 compression showed moderate agreement with 3DSSPP (ICC 0.74 and 0.58).

Data-driven musculoskeletal modelling · Research assistant project

Single-camera musculoskeletal analysis with deep-learning ground reaction force prediction

Lead developer of a markerless system for human movement analysis from ordinary single-camera video, requiring no markers, force plates or laboratory set-up. The system links video-derived kinematics with deep-learning ground reaction force prediction, inverse dynamics and muscle-activation estimation, with an adapted pose model that adds neck degrees of freedom. Fine-tuning a pretrained model for occupational lifting (low-rank adaptation with external-load conditioning) reduced vertical ground reaction force error by 46% relative to the zero-shot pretrained baseline (18.3% to 9.8% body weight RMSE) in 19-fold leave-one-subject-out cross-validation across 182 lifting trials, while training fewer than 1% of the model parameters (development-stage results).

Sports biomechanics · Master’s research

The effect of running economy in male runners wearing 3 types of footwear

The purpose of this study was to identify the effect of running economy in male runners wearing three types of racing footwear with different midsole bending stiffness. In twelve male middle-caliber runners, one racing shoe improved running economy and energy expenditure compared with another at 80% of the speed eliciting VO2max, but these improvements had no differences among the three types of footwear at 60% and 70% of vVO2max.

    DOIOpen access · CC BY
    Abstract

    Markerless motion capture has the potential to enhance ergonomic risk assessment, but evidence remains limited for occupational manual handling tasks of increasing complexity. This study compared a multi-view system (OpenCap) and a monocular system (MediaPipe) against a marker-based reference system (Vicon) for lifting kinematics. Outcomes were assessed using the Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA) risk assessment tool. Twenty healthy participants performed three two-handed lifting tasks of increasing complexity under controlled laboratory conditions. Errors increased by 15–30% from symmetrical to asymmetric multi-planar tasks, with both systems demonstrating lower error in the sagittal plane (RMSE = 9.9–31.2°) than in the frontal and transverse planes. Both systems were reliable for symmetrical lifting, including high REBA agreement, but validity declined for tasks involving trunk rotation and walking. MediaPipe showed lower error for trunk and proximal joints, whereas OpenCap performed better for distal kinematics, informing practical system selection.

    Keywords: markerless motion capture; manual handling; work-related musculoskeletal disorders. © 2026 The Authors, published by Elsevier Ltd under a CC BY licence.
  2. Zhang, M., Nieuwenhuys, A., & Zhang, Y.* (2025). Posture prediction models in digital human modeling for ergonomic design: A systematic review. Medical Engineering & Physics, 143, 104391.
    DOIOpen access · CC BY
    Abstract

    Posture prediction models have been widely used to support ergonomic design. This systematic review critically assessed the development, validation, and applications of posture prediction models in Digital Human Modeling (DHM). Following PRISMA guidelines, 24 studies were included from a search across nine academic databases, categorized into data-driven models (n = 12) and optimization-based models (n = 12). Data-driven models, particularly those employing neural network regression and artificial neural networks, demonstrated strong predictive accuracy and adaptability, but often lacked generalizability due to data imbalance and limited participant/task diversity. Optimization-based models, using algorithms such as gradient descent and genetic algorithms, showed high biomechanical fidelity but computational challenges and limited computer-aided design (CAD) integration. While a few models have been integrated with existing CAD software such as JACK and Santos™, most lacked ergonomic evaluation and real-time usability. Limitations identified include insufficient diverse datasets, computational inefficiencies, and limited validation in real-world conditions. Future research should prioritize model development supported by scalable motion data using computer vision-based technologies and hybrid strategies that combine learning-based inference with biomechanical simulation, offering a promising path toward achieving both accuracy and physiological realism in posture prediction.

    Keywords: digital human modeling; posture prediction; human factors; ergonomics; CAD integration; work-related injuries; workplace safety; computer vision-based technique. © 2025 The Author(s), published by Elsevier Ltd on behalf of IPEM under a CC BY licence.

研究

围绕这两个阶段，开发并评价了两个互补的工具：用于设计阶段的混合两阶段姿态预测模型，以及用于评估阶段的在线视频人体工效学评估系统。研究首先验证系统所依赖的无标记测量技术，在此基础上开发系统，再将系统输出与既定参考方法进行比较，并开展专家用户评估。

研究助理期间，主导开发基于普通单摄像头视频的无标记人体运动分析系统。该系统无需标记点、测力台或实验室环境，可用于步态分析、手工搬运风险评估（含腰椎负荷估算）以及康复评估中的初步筛查。

上述工作建立在中国北京体育大学的硕士研究基础之上，硕士课题为竞速跑鞋弯曲刚度对跑者跑步经济性及下肢生物力学特征的影响。

数字人体建模与姿态预测在工位或任务实际建立之前，为设计阶段的工效学评估预测作业姿态。无标记动作捕捉效度明确哪种姿态估计方法、在哪些关节上足以支持自动化工效学评估。视频工效学评估基于单个普通视频的姿势、手工搬运与生物力学风险评估。数据驱动的肌骨建模基于单摄像头视频的地面反作用力预测与肌骨分析。运动生物力学不同弯曲刚度竞速跑鞋下的跑步经济性与下肢生物力学。

数字人体建模中用于工效学设计的姿态预测模型：系统综述

本系统综述评估了数字人体建模（DHM）中姿态预测模型的开发、验证与应用。依据 PRISMA 指南检索九个学术数据库，纳入 24 项研究，分为数据驱动模型（n = 12）与优化模型（n = 12）。未来研究应优先发展结合学习推断与生物力学仿真的混合策略，以同时实现姿态预测的准确性与生理真实性。

数字人体建模 · 博士研究

面向手工物料搬运任务的混合两阶段姿态预测框架

本研究构建了将矢状面全身优化与三维上肢解析重建相结合的混合两阶段建模方法。第一阶段采用 5 个自由度的约束非线性优化，在任务与生物力学约束下最小化躯干力矩；第二阶段基于经验肱骨旋转约束，通过逆运动学解析重建 4 个自由度的上肢姿态。在 12 名受试者的垂直搬举、肩高钻孔与过肩钻孔任务中，与标记点动作捕捉比较，关节角度 RMSE 为 4.0°–37.5°，单个姿态总计算时间约 85 ms。模块化架构与低于 100 ms 的计算时间，可支持其整合进数字人体建模流程与早期工效学设计。

无标记动作捕捉 · 博士研究

无标记动作捕捉在职业搬举中的同期效度：运动学与工效学风险评估

本研究以标记点参考系统（Vicon）为标准，比较多视角系统（OpenCap）与单目系统（MediaPipe）的搬举运动学。20 名健康受试者在受控实验室条件下完成三类复杂度递增的双手搬举任务。由对称搬举到非对称多平面任务，误差增加 15–30%。两套系统在对称搬举中可靠，REBA 一致性高，但在涉及躯干旋转与行走的任务中效度下降。

视频工效学评估 · 博士研究

从记录板到摄像头：面向新西兰工作场所的在线视频人体工效学评估系统

在线视频人体工效学评估系统基于单个普通视频，采用无标记姿态估计分析作业任务，无需标记点、可穿戴传感器、测力台或实验室。同一视频可运行四个模块：RULA 与 REBA 姿势评分；NIOSH 推荐重量限值与搬举指数；静态关节力矩与 L5/S1 脊柱压缩力；以及完整动作周期的动态生物力学。REBA 风险等级在 132 次试验中与参考方法一致率为 84%，NIOSH 搬举指数平均绝对误差为 0.13，躯干力矩与 L5/S1 压缩力与 3DSSPP 呈中等一致性（ICC 0.74 与 0.58）。

数据驱动的肌骨建模 · 研究助理项目

基于单摄像头与深度学习地面反作用力预测的肌骨分析

主要开发者：基于普通单摄像头视频的无标记人体运动分析系统，无需标记点、测力台或实验室环境。系统将视频运动学与深度学习地面反作用力预测、逆动力学和肌肉激活估算相结合，并改进姿态模型、新增颈部自由度。针对职业搬举任务微调预训练模型（低秩适配与外部负荷条件输入），在 182 次搬举试验的 19 折留一受试者交叉验证中，垂直地面反作用力误差较零样本预训练基线降低 46%（RMSE 由 18.3% 体重降至 9.8% 体重），且仅训练不到 1% 的模型参数（开发阶段结果）。

男性跑者穿着三种跑鞋的跑步经济性
